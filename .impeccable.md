## Design Context

### Users
The primary user is the owner of a personal life-OS. Body is the central, wearable-independent health layer. Users scan health widgets frequently and customize their dashboard, data sources, widget sizes, and widget accent colors.

### Brand Personality
Personal, precise, and composed. Body should feel like a carefully designed health product rather than a generic analytics dashboard, clinical portal, or decorative wellness app.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use light or neutral card surfaces. Every widget has one user-selected accent color; shades of that single hue may be used freely. Additional semantic colors are reserved for genuinely universal states such as growth, warning, or danger. The design language must translate across rings, lines, tilemaps, timelines, and future visualization types.

### Design Principles
1. Treat typography and placement as the primary design system: titles, supporting copy, values, units, and KPIs need deliberate hierarchy and readable sizes.
2. Keep widget surfaces light and neutral; never turn the complete card into the accent color.
3. Use one accent-color family per widget. Do not introduce unrelated decorative colors.
4. Make the visualization meaningful, but design the surrounding information with equal care.
5. Every widget variant is deliberately designed across its supported footprints; added width or height must create useful compositional opportunity rather than merely stretching or crowding the 1×1 layout.
6. Use Tabler as the default icon family for Body; icon presence and treatment are evaluated per component.
7. Curate variants like products: add a variant only when it offers a meaningfully different and useful way to understand the same subject.
